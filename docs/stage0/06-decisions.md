The nonce-gap risk that made the MVP defer it is handled by the busy rule and the same-nonce cancel | — |

| D11 | **Notifications:** a separate, read-only notifier service; never affects contract state | Roadmap Stage 1 scope; a notice is a convenience, the contract is the authority | **Stage 2 (shipped):** per-person delivery by email (any SMTP, double opt-in, one-click unsubscribe), Telegram (one-time `t.me/<bot>?start=` link, `/stop`, blocked bot disables the chat) and webhooks (HMAC-SHA256 + timestamp, Slack/Discord text, SSRF guard), plus the operator feed. Each notice is sent only while it is still true, fanned out once per key to every recipient × active contact allowed by per-kind preferences; each delivery retries with exponential backoff on its own, gives up after a cap and is rate-limited per channel (at-least-once: receivers de-duplicate on `X-Behest-Delivery`). Plans watched: open releases, subscribers' own plans, plans a subscriber has a role in and asked to follow, `NOTIFY_OWNERS`. Later: SMS/push, digest mode, quiet hours, per-channel preferences |

| D15 | **ERC-20 adapter (Stage 2):** admin allowlist (≤ 4 reverting tokens; testnet WETH) stored outside the contract struct; transfers snapshot the token address; one fee reserve per vault transaction; same vault, nonce sequence, executor and recovery path as ETH | Reuses the proven ETH signing path. | D16 | **NEP-141 adapter (Stage 2):** admin allowlist (≤ 4; testnet wNEAR) with each token's registration price; deposits arrive by `ft_transfer_call` (msg = plan owner), anything unlisted or for a plan with a release open is refunded by the token; escrow per token and plan under raw keys; split like NEAR among heirs with a NEAR destination; the contract registers each heir with the token (NEP-145) before paying, from NEAR reserved out of the plan at proposal time; listing a token registers the contract itself | Heirs need nothing, not even a token registration. No migration. Same claim/retry/cancel paths as NEAR | Reading registration price from the token at proposal time; per-plan token opt-in |
| D17 | **Release rules (Stage 2):** optional per-plan rules beside the plan (raw key, owner-set, frozen while a release is open, re-checked when heirs change): fixed amounts paid first in listed order and capped by what's there, then shares; a named heir receives each asset's rounding remainder (otherwise it stays, D12); a heir's share of 0 is now allowed. Unclaimed shares: a transfer still `Pending` one claim window after the plan becomes claimable can be moved by anyone to the heir's named alternate, and one window later to the plan's fallback. Only never-signed transfers move, so no ETH nonce or signature exists. Alternates and the fallback must be outsiders, so the current holder shows which step a transfer is at | Owners can express "100 NEAR to my nephew, the rest by share" and "if she never claims, to her brother, then to charity" without trusting anyone to redirect funds. Fixed and remainder amounts join the heir's single per-asset transfer, so no extra vault transactions or fee reserve | Redistributing unclaimed shares among the other heirs pro rata; reassigning transfers that failed rather than went unclaimed |
| D18 | **Delay signers (Stage 2):** optional per-plan set (≤ 10; never the owner or a heir, may overlap guardians and the UI says so) with its own quorum. While an approved release is waiting, one signer requests an extension (≤ `max_extension_ms`, optional public reason code ≤ 64 chars) and it applies when the quorum agrees; total extensions per proposal ≤ `max_total_extension_ms` (≤ 365 days). One request at a time; votes don't carry into a new proposal. The extension moves `waiting_ends_at_ms`, which is the owner's cancel window, the signing cutoff and the start of claiming | Matches the vision: a separate quorum that can only delay, within contract caps. Shortening is deliberately absent: the vision limits this role to extending. Reusing the single cutoff field keeps "nothing is signed before the owner's window ends" true by construction | Shortening a delay; evidence attachments; per-asset delays |
| D19 | **Release mode (Stage 2):** per plan, `Claim` (default), `Automatic` or `Hybrid` with a grace period, plus ordered overrides for one heir (or every heir) and an asset group (all, NEAR-side, Ethereum-side, Bitcoin); the first matching override decides each transfer. A share reassigned to an alternate or the fallback follows the asset-group or plan mode. In Automatic (from the end of waiting) or Hybrid (after the grace) anyone may call `release(owner, heir)`, which starts that heir's still-pending transfers exactly as a claim would; the executor does this every tick. Heirs can always claim themselves. Destinations and amounts stay the approved ones | Heirs who never open the app still receive. The trigger carries no authority: it can only start what guardians approved, after the owner's window | Arbitrary per-asset lists instead of the two chain groups; automatic retry of failed NEAR transfers by third parties |
| D20 | **Bitcoin testnet adapter (Stage 2):** each plan can register a Bitcoin vault: a P2WPKH address of an MPC key at `behest/v1/<owner>/btc` (anyone may call `register_btc_vault`, which asks the MPC contract for the derived key once and stores it; the contract needs the key hash for the sighash). The owner names testnet SegWit destinations (`tb1q…` v0, `tb1p…` v1 Taproot; checksums verified on-chain) for heirs, alternates or the fallback. The proposer reports up to 2 confirmed vault coins (like `eth_balance`); the proposal reserves fees for every payout at the admin fee-rate cap (`btc.max_fee_rate`, testnet 5 sat/vB): the first payout spends the reported coins, each later one a single change output. The rest is split by the release rules like any asset; a share below 330 sats stays in the vault. One payout per heir, built and hashed on-chain (BIP143, version 2, RBF-signalling), one MPC signature per input, DER low-S. Payouts are chained like an ETH nonce: whichever is signed first spends the coins and the next spends its change, so heirs can claim in any order before anything is mined. A re-sign after a signing failure rebuilds the identical tx; after a network failure only the latest payout may be re-signed with a higher fee, from its change. Each replaced version is kept (`get_btc_tx_versions`), because it can still be mined: the executor broadcasts a bump as its replacement, and when the network holds an earlier version instead it reports that txid; the contract accepts only versions it built, makes the reported one current, moves the release head to its change, and resets any later payout that spent the bumped change (`Rechained`, signed again from the real head). A Bitcoin withdrawal with a replaced version can't be cancelled. A guardian can rebase unsent payouts onto new coins (`rebase_btc`, only while nothing was broadcast, and the new set must keep one old coin so earlier signed versions are void). The owner withdraws BTC with `withdraw_btc` (D7 rules: no release open, change to the vault, one at a time). The executor broadcasts through Esplora (testnet4) after checking the raw tx against the contract's record, waits for each input's parent, and reports `Conflict` when a vault coin was spent by anything else | Same trust model as ETH: the contract alone decides inputs, outputs and amounts and holds every signed byte; the executor only relays. Reported coins can't be stolen from (outputs are fixed); a false or spent coin only makes signing or broadcast fail, which a guardian fixes by rebasing. Chaining keeps one signature per input and no extra fee reserve beyond one tx per heir | Reading coins from a light client instead of reports; more than 2 inputs per payout; mainnet |
| D21 | **Follow-up release rounds:** a settled release is archived (history of the last 5 via `get_release_history`) on the plan's next check-in, deposit, plan edit or proposal, instead of blocking all of them. While the owner hasn't checked in since a settled release, guardians **and heirs** may propose a follow-up round for whatever is left (later deposits, an under-reported balance, the unused fee reserve); guardians approve it with the normal quorum and the owner can cancel during the waiting period. Fixed amounts are recorded as paid when their round settles (`get_fixed_paid`) and are not paid again; a new plan version or new rules reset that record. Each proposal records the balances it was built from (`get_release_snapshot`). Rejection now unwinds like a cancel | The MVP snapshot was final: an under-report (threat 4), later deposits (D5) and anything left after a release were stranded once the owner was gone, and a returning owner couldn't even check in. Heirs are the ones hurt by an under-report, so they can start the fix; guardians still vet the numbers | Reading balances from light clients instead of reports; more than 5 rounds of history on-chain (events keep the full record) |
| D22 | **Proven Sepolia outcomes:** the contract verifies a transaction's receipt status itself. A proof is the block header (RLP), the transaction index and Merkle-Patricia proofs of the transaction and receipt; the contract checks them, matches the transaction hash to a version it signed for the transfer's current nonce, then asks the Rainbow Bridge Ethereum light client on NEAR (`block_hash_safe`) whether the header is final. A proven revert is required for `reissue_reverted_transfer` (now callable by anyone); a proven success confirms the transfer. The executor waits for light-client finality, builds the proof from any Sepolia node (`ethproof.ts`) and re-issues by itself | Re-issuing under a new nonce is the one executor/admin action that could pay twice (threat 20); a proof removes the trust. The light client is already deployed and maintained on testnet and mainnet, so no new trusted party is added | An admin-attested re-issue (the MVP); a quorum of executors; waiting for an independent zk receipt prover |
| D23 | **EVM wallets through contract-verified signatures:** an EVM wallet (MetaMask, Rabby, WalletConnect…) signs a readable `personal_sign` (EIP-191) message: `Behest signed action`, a one-line summary for the human (not checked), a blank line, then one JSON object `{contract, network, actor, method, args, nonce, deadline_ms}`. `evm_call(message, signature)` (anyone may submit it; the relayer does) checks the header and strict JSON (unknown or duplicate fields refused, so what was signed is exactly what runs), `contract` = this account, `network` = testnet/mainnet from the account suffix, a canonical 65-byte signature (`v` 27/28 or 0/1; zero, out-of-range and high-S refused; `ecrecover` with the malleability flag), `actor` = the recovered address, `nonce` = the actor's `get_evm_nonce` (sequential, decimal without leading zeros, used up on success), `deadline_ms` not passed. It then runs one allowlisted method with `caller()` returning the actor's eth-implicit account (`0x` + 40 lowercase hex), and emits `evm_call {actor, method, nonce}`. **Allowed:** everything the relayer sponsors for heirs, guardians, delay signers and owners (`claim`, `retry_transfer`, `propose_release`, `approve_release`, `reject_release`, `check_in`, `cancel_release`, `reassign_unclaimed`, `request_extension`, `approve_extension`, `release`, `register_btc_vault`, `rebase_btc`); the owner configuration that only needs storage, paid under D24 (`set_plan`, `set_release_rules`, `set_release_options`, `set_btc_destinations`); and the owner's withdrawals (`withdraw` and `withdraw_ft` pay only the owner's own 0x account; `withdraw_vault` and `withdraw_btc` pay a destination written in the signed text; `cancel_vault_withdrawal` of the owner's own withdrawal). None of these needs an attached deposit (the NEAR path has no 1-yocto check on them either), and without them an EVM owner could never take funds back. **Never:** `deposit` and anything needing real NEAR, admin and executor methods, callbacks, `evm_call` itself. The override is set by a guard after verification and cleared on return or unwind; callbacks are separate receipts and never see it; a self-call can't carry it; admin and executor checks refuse under it, so a signature never makes anyone admin or executor. Nonces live under raw keys (`evmn:`), no migration. Payouts to a 0x heir create the eth-implicit account | NEAR's own MetaMask path (NEP-518 `rlp_execute` via a chain-398 RPC) can't be sponsored: MetaMask has no `eth_signTransaction`, so the relayer could never pay the gas. A signature verified in the contract keeps EVM users gas-free like everyone else, needs no extra trusted party (the contract checks every byte), and the readable message shows the exact call in the wallet | EIP-712 typed data for richer wallet display; parallel nonces; delegating an EVM key to a NEAR key |
| D24 | **Sponsored plan storage:** a sponsor pool in the contract (`fund_sponsor_pool`, payable by anyone; `get_sponsorship(owner) → {pool, allowance_left, daily_left}`). An owner call that grows storage with **no attached deposit**, in a transaction signed by the admin-registered relayer (`set_sponsor_config {relayer, allowance_per_owner, daily_new_plans}`; `signer_account_id`, which covers NEP-366 meta-transactions and `evm_call` alike), is paid from the pool within a per-owner lifetime allowance (default 0.1 NEAR, never restored) and a global daily cap on sponsored **new** plans (default 50/UTC day); otherwise `E_SPONSOR_EXHAUSTED` and the owner can pay. The bookkeeping records are written before storage is measured, so their bytes are part of the cost. Freed storage returns to the pool, up to the bytes the pool backs for that owner; the owner never receives pool money (freed storage was never refunded to owners). The relayer now also sponsors `set_plan` and the owner setters as meta-transactions with 0 deposit (`set_plan` needs no existing plan). State under raw keys (`sponsor_cfg`, `sponsor_pool`, `sponsor_day`, `spo:`), no migration. Testnet pool: 1 NEAR from the admin | Owners shouldn't need NEAR to write a will, least of all EVM owners who can't attach any. Caps bound what a flood of fresh accounts can take (≤ 50 new plans × allowance per day) | An upfront owner fee priced by policy complexity funds the pool; per-owner allowance tied to plan value |
| D25 | **Plan tiers paid once, upfront, in USDC:** an admin schedule (`set_tier_config {fee_token, fee_symbol, fee_decimals, tiers}`; default **Free** $0: 1 heir, ≤ 3 guardians, NEAR only, claim mode; **Standard** $49: ≤ 5 heirs and guardians, Ethereum/Bitcoin destinations and NEP-141 escrow, release rules; **Complete** $149: everything). The schedule must start free, rise strictly in price, never shrink a limit, and end with a tier that allows every valid plan; the fee token can change only while the treasury has never collected. Payment is `ft_transfer_call` of the fee token to Behest with `msg {"pay_tier":"<owner>"}`, **from anyone** (owner, relative, NEAR Intents' 1Click with `customRecipientMsg` on mainnet), for any account, with or without a plan. Every payment is credit (`paid` never decreases): the owner holds the highest tier its credit covers, kept even if prices rise, so an upgrade costs only the difference and an overpayment counts toward the next tier rather than bouncing back to an intermediary. A first payment for an account must be ≥ 1 whole token (otherwise refunded), malformed messages and other tokens are refunded. **Gate:** `set_plan`, `set_release_rules`, `set_release_options`, `set_btc_destinations` and NEP-141 escrow deposits refuse (`E_TIER_REQUIRED: this plan needs the <name> tier`; deposits are refunded) a configuration needing more than max(paid tier, the tier the saved configuration already needs), so plans saved before tiers keep their level and simplifying never refunds. Tiers never touch check-in, proposals, votes, claims, releases or transfers. **Accounting:** fee revenue is a treasury record (`get_treasury → {collected, withdrawn, pending, balance, payments}`) apart from plan escrow, chain-fee reserves and the D24 sponsor pool; only the admin pays it out (`withdraw_treasury {amount, to}`, restored if the token transfer fails). Views `get_tier {owner} → {tier, paid, required, allowance, due[], features}`, `get_tier_config`, `required_tier {plan, rules, options, btc_destinations, token_escrow}`. With no fee token set, tiers are off. State under raw keys (`tier_cfg`, `tier:`, `treasury`), no migration. Testnet: a mock USDC with a public faucet (`contracts/mock-usdc`) | One token every kind of owner can reach: USDC exists on most chains and NEAR Intents swaps almost anything into USDC on NEAR, so EVM and Bitcoin holders pay without NEAR. Fixed dollar prices need no oracle. Tiers are easier to understand than per-item pricing and the contract can enforce them by counting what a plan uses. Gating saves only keeps the inheritance itself independent of payment. Crediting instead of refunding protects payers going through an intermediary that can't take a refund | Price per year instead of once; tier allowance for the D24 sponsor pool; pay in NEAR at an oracle price; refunds on downgrade |
| D26 | **DAOs and multisigs in any role.** Behest authorizes the calling account (`predecessor`, or the EVM actor under D23), never the signer, except the relayer check for D24 storage. So a SputnikDAO v2 account (what Trezu manages) can own, guard, inherit or delay-sign with no special case: its calls are `add_proposal` FunctionCall proposals that run as the DAO when the council's last vote executes them, with deposits paid from the DAO's balance. Added: **check-in delegates**, up to 10 accounts the owner names (`set_checkin_delegates`, payable, frozen while a release is open; `get_checkin_delegates`) that may call `check_in {owner}` and nothing else, kept in their own storage entry (`cid:<owner>`) so no plan migrates. A delegate's check-in is refused while a release is open (it would cancel it). Delegates are a **Standard** tier feature (`TierSpec.checkin_delegates`; the schedule moved to a versioned key `tier_cfg2`, the D25 layout under `tier_cfg` is read and converted with delegates = release rules). The relayer sponsors a delegate's `check_in` for the plan it names. The web app adds an "Act as a DAO" session: a council member signs in with a NEAR wallet and picks the DAO; roles and powers come from `get_policy`; every call becomes `add_proposal` with the bond and a plain-words description (several owner calls, e.g. `set_plan` + `deposit`, go in one proposal); calls to other contracts work the same way (the DAO pays its tier with USDC `ft_transfer_call`); the plan screen lists the DAO's open Behest proposals with Approve / Reject (`act_proposal`) and Trezu links. DAO-owned plans attach their own storage deposit (D24's pool only covers transactions the relayer signs; a DAO's action is signed by the member who casts the deciding vote), and the wizard says so before anything is signed. It also warns when the check-in interval isn't well above the DAO's `proposal_period` (suggesting delegates) and when a DAO heir would have to claim. The notifier can send a DAO's notices to every current council member (`PUT /v1/council`, signed by a member; membership read from `get_policy`) | A family trust, foundation or company treasury holds funds in a multisig, and should be able to plan for its signers disappearing, or inherit, like a person. Proposals take days, so a DAO can't check in every few weeks on its own: delegates keep the plan alive without being able to touch it. Nothing in the release path changed, so every existing guarantee holds for DAOs | Other multisig contracts (Safe-style NEAR multisigs) work the same way but have no picker yet. Trezu doesn't list testnet DAOs, so its links only work on mainnet |
| D27 | **Heirs can receive through NEAR Intents.** Per recipient (heirs, named alternates and the fallback) the owner can opt in with `set_intents_delivery([{account_id, enabled, intents_account}])` (payable, owner, frozen while a release is open, stored beside the plan under `idl:<owner>` so nothing migrates; `intents_account` defaults to the NEAR destination and may be a `0x…` address). An opted-in recipient's NEAR and NEP-141 transfers are paid into their account inside the Intents verifier instead of their wallet. NEAR goes as **one receipt** to wNEAR with two actions, `near_deposit` then `ft_transfer_call` to the verifier with msg `{"receiver_id": <intents account>}`; batched actions are atomic, so a failed wrap never touches other plans' wNEAR escrow. NEP-141 shares go from escrow with `ft_transfer_call` straight to the verifier, which Behest registers with each token once. `ft_transfer_call` returns what the verifier kept: **any refund is a failure**. The transfer goes `Failed` (`IntentsRefunded`, `IntentsPartlyRefunded` or `PromiseFailed`), what did arrive is recorded per transfer (`get_intents_progress`: `paid`, plus wNEAR `wrapped` and handed back, which is unwrapped into escrow with `near_withdraw`), and the normal retry path sends only the unpaid rest without wrapping again what is already held. A result claiming more than was sent counts only what was sent. Admin: `set_intents_config({verifier, wnear} \| None)` registers Behest and the verifier with wNEAR once each and refunds the rest of the deposit; `None` switches delivery off and retries then pay wallets directly, still only the unpaid rest. Intents delivery is a **Standard** feature, gated with the other cross-chain delivery (`external_assets`, so the tier schedule's layout doesn't change). ETH and Bitcoin need no contract change: on mainnet the app fills in a NEAR Intents bridge deposit address as the heir's normal destination, so the vault payout lands in Intents. The heir screen shows the Intents balance (`mt_batch_balance_of`), withdraws (a NEAR account calls the verifier's `ft_withdraw`; a `0x` account signs an `ft_withdraw` intent) and, on mainnet, swaps through 1Click with `depositType: "INTENTS"` and a signed transfer intent (NEP-413 for NEAR wallets, ERC-191 for Ethereum wallets) and no `appFees` | Heirs often want something other than NEAR, on another chain, or to hold value without managing a new wallet's tokens and storage. Intents lets one inheritance land in one account that can become anything later, with no Behest fee on what heirs do with it. Treating refunds as failures keeps "Confirmed" meaning the heir really has it | On testnet the verifier is a mock (`contracts/mock-intents`: deposits, `mt_` balances, configurable refunds, `ft_withdraw`; no intents, signatures, swaps or bridge). The real `intents.near`, its signed-intent formats, the PoA bridge and 1Click are **untested until a later mainnet step** |

Production timer values, attestations, guardian replacement while inactive, privacy modes, and legal review. (ERC-20, NEP-141, release rules, delay signers, release modes and Bitcoin testnet moved into Stage 2 work: D15–D20.) See the roadmap stages.
