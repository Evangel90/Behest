# 05 · Threat model (MVP, testnet)

Severity is for a mainnet deployment of the same design. Status says what the MVP does about it.

| # | Threat | Impact | Mitigation | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Owner key compromised | Attacker edits heirs and withdraws | D28: with an edit notice set, heir, destination, allocation, guardian, quorum and release-mode edits wait out the notice (guardians and heirs are told) and the owner can cancel; lowering the notice also waits. Withdrawals stay immediate (the owner's own assets) | Mitigated when the owner sets a notice; withdrawals accepted |
| 2 | Guardian collusion (≥ quorum) while owner is alive | Premature release | Release requires `Overdue` first (owner must miss check-ins), then a waiting period during which one owner check-in cancels it. Funds can only go to the owner's chosen heirs | Mitigated |
| 3 | Guardians disappear (quorum unreachable) | Funds stuck | Never auto-lower quorum. Owner can replace guardians while active. D29: standby guardians fill resigned seats at once and unresponsive ones (past the availability window) after a swap delay; `get_guardian_status` and notices flag a plan short of quorum, which waits paused for a standby, the owner or a guardian | Mitigated with standbys; otherwise paused (documented) |
| 4 | Guardian under-reports ETH, ERC-20 or BTC balance in the snapshot | Heirs receive less; remainder stays in vault | Nothing is redirected. The contract records every reported balance (`get_release_snapshot`) and the web app shows it next to the live vault before anyone votes, warning on any gap (over-reports too, which would make transfers fail). Whatever stays behind goes out in a follow-up round that heirs can start themselves (D21) | Mitigated |
| 5 | Malicious / compromised executor | False status reports, refusing to broadcast | Executor cannot alter payloads; signed tx is public in contract state so anyone can broadcast; executors are replaceable by admin | Mitigated |
| 6 | Replay / duplicate execution | Double payment | One fixed nonce per ETH transfer; NEAR transfers move to in-flight before the promise; callbacks are private | Mitigated (tested) |
| 7 | Signature before cancellation window ends | Irreversible early release | Contract checks `now >= waiting_ends_at` before every `sign`; `check_in`/`cancel` rejected from that instant | Mitigated (tested) |
| 8 | Payload substitution (recipient/amount/chain) | Funds redirected | Payload built inside contract from snapshot; no caller-supplied fields except a fee bump bounded by the cap | Mitigated (tested) |
| 9 | Proposal snapshot stale vs policy edit | Release to old heirs | Edits rejected while a proposal is open; proposal pins `plan_version` | Mitigated |
| 10 | Fee spike / fee exhaustion | Transfer stalls | Fee reserve at proposal; `FeeAboveCap` → retry later; funds stay in vault | Mitigated |
| 11 | Sepolia reorg | Status says Confirmed for a dropped tx | Executor waits N confirmations (default 6); retry reuses the same nonce | Mitigated |
| 12 | MPC timeout / failure | Transfer stuck in `Signing` | Callback failure → `Failed(SignFailed)`, retryable with the same nonce | Mitigated |
| 13 | Contract upgrade key compromised | Total loss | MVP uses a single testnet full-access key. Stage 4: time-locked multi-party upgrades, or lock the contract | **Accepted (testnet only)** |
| 14 | Admin abuse | Pause griefing; a false re-issue (see 20) | Admin can pause and re-issue a reverted transfer to its fixed heir and amount; no method changes recipients, amounts or plans | Partly (MVP) |
| 15 | Frontend compromised | User signs a malicious `set_plan` | Plain-language and rule view show the exact plan; wallet shows the call. Stage 3: signed builds | Partly |
| 16 | Demo timers in production | Releases in minutes | Timer bounds compiled in by feature flag; production build rejects demo durations (tested) | Mitigated |
| 17 | Heir loses destination key | Share unrecoverable | Destination fixed by owner; alternates/fallback (D17). D30: a heir proves control while it still holds the key, so a dead address shows as unproven; D31: while the owner is active the heir asks for a new destination and the owner approves | Mitigated while the owner is active; accepted after |
| 18 | Unbounded storage growth / spam | Storage DoS | Caps on heirs/guardians; owner pays storage deposit at plan creation | Mitigated |
| 19 | Destination-chain gas rule change | Transfer reverts for out-of-gas; fee spent, nonce used | Configurable gas limit snapshotted per proposal; executor estimates before first broadcast and reports `GasLimitTooLow` instead of sending; same-nonce re-sign with a higher limit | Mitigated |
| 20 | A transfer is re-issued that didn't really revert | The original (still valid) and the re-issue could both be mined, paying that heir twice from the vault | **D22:** `reissue_reverted_transfer` needs a recorded proof of the revert. `prove_eth_outcome` checks Merkle-Patricia proofs of the transaction and its receipt against a block header, that the transaction is one the contract signed for the transfer's current nonce, and that the light client (`client-eth2.sepolia.testnet`) holds the header's hash as finalized. Anyone may prove or re-issue; a proof of success confirms the transfer instead, correcting a false "Reverted" report. Live `e2e-revert` | Mitigated (trusts the Ethereum light client's sync committee, not the admin or executor) |
| 21 | Malicious or non-standard ERC-20 | A token that returns `false` instead of reverting would look delivered when it wasn't. A token contract could also misbehave in other ways | Admin allowlist of at most 4 known, reverting tokens. Transfers snapshot the token address. The executor checks `to`, `value` and calldata against the snapshot | Mitigated (allowlist) |
| 22 | Guardian misreports a token balance | Over-report: that heir's token transfer can't be paid yet, and later vault nonces wait behind it. Under-report: tokens stay in the vault | Same model as `eth_balance` (D5). Guardians approve the exact transfer list, and anyone can check the vault. The executor simulates before broadcasting and reports `WouldRevert` without spending a fee. After a top-up, the heir retries at the same nonce, with no admin trust (live: `e2e-partial`) | Partly (MVP) |
| 23 | Owner withdrawal used to race or block a release | Withdrawing during a release would change heirs' amounts. An unresolved withdrawal could hold a nonce ahead of heirs' transfers | No withdrawal while a release is open (`E_PROPOSAL_OPEN`); no proposal while a withdrawal is unresolved (`E_VAULT_BUSY`). Executors resolve withdrawals without the owner, including a same-nonce 0-ETH cancel for one that can't go through | Mitigated |
| 24 | NEP-141 token misbehaves or registration price changes | A token could take deposits but fail transfers, or charge more to register a heir than was reserved | Admin allowlist (≤ 4). A failed token transfer stays `Failed` and is retried by the heir like NEAR; escrow isn't released until it confirms. Registration is attempted once per transfer and only if the reserve covers it; any unused reserve returns to the plan when it settles, and a cancel returns all of it. If a heir is already registered, the token refunds the deposit to the contract (not the plan): a small, bounded leak | Mitigated (allowlist) |
| 25 | Unclaimed share moved away from a slow heir | Anyone can move a heir's untouched share to the named alternate once the claim window passes, so a heir who is late loses it | Only to recipients the owner named, only after the owner-chosen window (production minimum 30 days, measured from when claiming opened), only for transfers never started; a claimed or failed transfer never moves. Rules are visible to guardians before they approve and can't change while a release is open | Accepted (owner's choice) |
| 26 | Delay signers stall a release | Colluding delay signers keep pushing the release back | They can only extend, never approve, cancel or redirect. Each extension needs their quorum and is capped; the total per proposal is capped (owner-chosen, ≤ 365 days). Every extension is public (`waiting_extended`) and notified to owner, guardians and heirs | Mitigated (bounded) |
| 27 | Third party triggers an automatic release | Someone starts heirs' transfers at a moment of their choosing | Only in modes the owner chose, only after the (possibly extended) owner window and any hybrid grace, and only the approved transfers to the approved destinations. Starting a transfer early is impossible; late just means the executor was down, and heirs can still claim | Mitigated |
| 28 | False or stale Bitcoin coins reported | A guardian reports coins the vault doesn't have, or that were spent, or overstates their value | Outputs (heirs, amounts, change to the vault) are fixed by the contract and the value is committed in every BIP143 signature, so a wrong value only produces an invalid tx; nothing can pay anyone else. Guardians approve the reported coins with the proposal. The executor checks each input before broadcasting and reports `Conflict`; a guardian rebases onto the real coins while nothing was broadcast | Mitigated |
| 29 | Two versions of a Bitcoin payout both confirm | A re-signed or rebased payout and an older signed version are both mined, paying a heir twice | A re-sign keeps the same inputs (identical tx or an RBF bump), so only one version can be mined. A rebase is refused once anything was broadcast and must reuse one of the old coins, so every new payout conflicts with every older one. Chained payouts reference the parent's txid, which is fixed before signing (SegWit) | Mitigated |
| 30 | Bitcoin payout stuck at a low fee | Fee spikes leave an earlier chained payout unconfirmed | Fees are reserved at the admin cap; the latest payout can be fee-bumped from its change (RBF), and an earlier one confirms once a later child pays enough (CPFP effect) or the network clears. Testnet only; the cap is an admin setting | Accepted (testnet) |
| 31 | A fee bump's earlier version is mined | The executor or contract treats the original as a foreign spend, cancels the withdrawal or strands later payouts | Replaced versions are kept on-chain; the executor reports whichever version the network holds and the contract adopts it only if it built it, moving the head to its change and re-signing later payouts from there. A withdrawal with a replaced version can't be cancelled | Mitigated |
| 32 | Contact details leak | Someone learns an owner's, guardian's or heir's email, chat or endpoint, linking them to a plan | Contacts never go on-chain; they live only in the notifier's database and are returned only to a session signed by that account. Logs carry account ids, never addresses. Webhook secrets are shown once. Operator can read the database (trusted for privacy, not for funds) | Mitigated (operator trusted) |
| 33 | Signed notifier request replayed or redirected | A captured signature re-adds a contact, or a signature made for another app is reused | NEP-413 recipient must be this notifier, the signed JSON carries account, action, arguments and a timestamp checked to ±5 min, and each 32-byte nonce is accepted once. Arguments come only from the signed text. Keys must be full-access keys of the account (RPC), or an unused implicit account's own key | Mitigated |
| 34 | Notifier made to call internal services (SSRF) | A webhook URL points at cloud metadata, localhost or the LAN | https only, ports 443/8443, no credentials in URLs, no redirects followed; every resolved address must be public, checked inside the socket's DNS lookup so a rebinding name can't switch to a private address after the check. Only `NOTIFY_WEBHOOK_ALLOW_INSECURE` (local development) relaxes it | Mitigated |
| 35 | Forged webhook calls | Someone posts fake "ready to receive" notices to a family's Slack or a heir's automation | Each subscription has its own secret; the body is signed with HMAC-SHA256 over `timestamp.body` and receivers reject stale timestamps. Notices only inform: acting on one still needs the contract, which can't be fooled by a message | Mitigated |
| 36 | Telegram link token stolen | Whoever opens the `t.me/…?start=` link first binds their chat to the victim's account and reads its notices | Tokens are random (192 bits), single-use, expire in 15 minutes, are stored hashed, and are issued only to a signed request. The bot replies with the linked account, the account's other channels are told a chat was linked, and the settings screen lists every chat with remove; `/stop` unlinks | Mitigated |
| 37 | Email bombing through confirmation mails | An attacker makes the notifier mail a victim repeatedly | Each confirmation needs a fresh account signature; at most 3 confirmation mails per address and 10 per account per day, per-IP and per-account request limits, and nothing but the confirmation is sent until the link is confirmed (a button, so link scanners can't confirm). Every notice has one-click unsubscribe | Mitigated |
| 38 | Notifier down or a notice lost | An owner misses a reminder; a heir doesn't hear a release is ready | Notifications never change contract state or deadlines; the web app shows everything. Deliveries retry with backoff and are recorded; restarts resume from the database. Owners are told not to rely on reminders alone | Accepted (by design) |
| 39 | EVM signature forged, replayed or reused elsewhere | Someone acts as an EVM owner, guardian or heir: votes, claims, edits a plan or withdraws | `ecrecover` in the contract over the EIP-191 hash of the exact text; the JSON binds contract id, network, actor (must equal the recovered address), a per-actor sequential nonce and a deadline. A replay fails the nonce; another deployment or network fails the contract/network check; the notifier's EIP-191 texts start differently and carry `app: behest-notifier`, so neither can be used as the other. The summary line is unchecked, but the wallet shows the JSON that runs. Live: `e2e-evm` replays a check-in at the relayer and directly at the contract | Mitigated |
| 40 | Signature malleability | A second encoding of the same signature slips past single-use checks | Only 65 bytes, `v` 27/28 or 0/1, nonzero `r` < n and `s` ≤ n/2 (also `ecrecover`'s malleability flag); the relayer and notifier apply the same rule. Replay protection doesn't depend on the signature bytes anyway: the contract uses the nonce, the notifier the message hash | Mitigated |
| 41 | Acting-as override leaks | A later call, a callback or a privileged check runs as the EVM actor | The override lives in a guard set only after verification and cleared on return and on unwind; nested `evm_call` and self-calls are refused; callbacks are separate receipts (fresh instance) and `#[private]` checks the real predecessor. Admin and executor checks read the real predecessor and refuse outright under the override; the allowlist excludes every admin, executor and callback method (unit tests) | Mitigated |
| 42 | Sponsor pool drained | Fresh accounts or EVM keys create plans until the pool is empty; an owner frees pool-paid storage to pocket it | Only transactions signed by the registered relayer, which checks roles and rate-limits per sender and globally; per-owner lifetime allowance (not restored by freeing storage); daily cap on sponsored new plans; freed storage returns to the pool, never to the owner. Worst case per day: the cap × the allowance. If the relayer key signs other transactions that reach Behest, those can draw on the pool within the same caps, so it must sign only Behest calls. Exhaustion only means owners pay their own storage | Mitigated (bounded) |
| 43 | Nonce storage spam | Anyone with throwaway keys makes the contract store nonces | A nonce is written only when the dispatched method succeeds, which needs a real role in a plan or a sponsored (capped) new plan; ≈ 100 bytes each, paid by the contract | Accepted (bounded) |
| 44 | Tier bypass | An owner saves a plan beyond what was paid by splitting the change across calls, or by depositing tokens before paying | Every owner setter and NEP-141 escrow deposit compares the whole resulting configuration (plan, rules, options, Bitcoin destinations, token escrow) with max(paid tier, the tier the saved configuration already needs); the client copy of the rule is advisory only. Plans saved before tiers keep their level but can't add more (unit tests, live `e2e-tiers`) | Mitigated |
| 45 | Payments lost or misdirected | A payment through an intermediary (NEAR Intents) bounces, or credits the wrong account; spam payments make the contract store records | Valid payments are always kept as credit (never refunded to an intermediary that can't receive it); only malformed messages, other tokens and a first payment under one whole token are refunded by the token. The owner named in the message is credited, whoever paid, and paying never changes a plan. Records are ≈ 100 bytes and cost the spammer at least one USDC each | Mitigated |
| 46 | Fee revenue mixed with heirs' funds | Treasury payouts draw on plan escrow, chain-fee reserves or the sponsor pool | The treasury is its own record in the fee token only; `withdraw_treasury` is admin-only, limited to collected − withdrawn − pending, and restores the balance if the token transfer fails. No release path reads or writes it | Mitigated |
| 47 | Check-in delegate abuse | A delegate (or a stolen delegate key) keeps a plan alive after the owner is gone, delaying the heirs; or tries to do more than check in | A delegate can only call `check_in {owner}`: every other owner method acts on the caller's own plan, so a delegate reaching them gets `E_NO_PLAN` (tested for withdraw, cancel, edits, delegates). It can't cancel a release: its check-in is refused while one is open. The owner names and removes delegates (≤ 10, never the owner, frozen during a release), every delegate check-in is an event with `by`, and the notifier reports check-ins. A delegate that keeps checking in after the owner is gone only delays heirs; owners should pick people who would tell the guardians, and remove delegates who leave the DAO's council | Partly; documented |
| 48 | DAO proposals mislead voters | A council member's proposal says one thing and calls another, or a stale proposal runs long after it was written | The app builds the description from the exact call it encodes and lists each action; Behest's own checks still apply when it runs (frozen plans, tier, roles). Voters in other apps see the raw FunctionCall. Sputnik expires proposals after `proposal_period` | Partly; documented |
| 49 | DAO plans fail at storage | A DAO-owned plan is saved with no deposit, expecting D24 sponsorship, and the proposal fails after the vote | The app always attaches the storage deposit for DAO sessions and says so up front; the relayer never sees DAO transactions. A failed proposal is marked `Failed` by Sputnik and changes nothing in Behest | Mitigated |
| 50 | Intents verifier refunds | The verifier keeps only part of a payment (or none, or the call fails), and Behest marks the heir paid, pays twice on retry, or strands the refunded wNEAR | `ft_transfer_call`'s result is the amount kept; anything less than sent leaves the transfer `Failed` and records only what arrived (`paid`), so a retry sends the unpaid rest. Refunded wNEAR is unwrapped back to escrow; if unwrapping fails it stays recorded as `wrapped` and the retry sends it as it is instead of wrapping again. A result above what was sent counts only what was sent. NEAR is wrapped and sent in one atomic receipt, so a failure can't spend other plans' wNEAR. Unit tests cover full, partial and over-reported results, failed batches and failed unwraps; `e2e-intents` runs failing, half-refunding and keeping verifiers live (mock) | Mitigated (mock only) |
| 51 | Misconfigured or hostile verifier | The admin points `set_intents_config` at the wrong contract (or a verifier that keeps tokens without crediting the heir), or wNEAR is the wrong token | Only the admin sets it; the change is an event. Heirs see their Intents balance on their screen and can withdraw. If the verifier misbehaves, `set_intents_config(None)` switches delivery off and every retry pays wallets directly (tested), still only the unpaid rest. A verifier that keeps tokens without crediting anyone looks like success to Behest: this is trust in the configured verifier, documented, and why the mainnet value must be `intents.near` | Partly; documented |
| 52 | Griefer unseats an active guardian | Someone calls `request_standby` to swap out a guardian who is merely quiet | Only guardians past the owner-chosen availability window (≥ 30 days) or resigned qualify; the swap waits `swap_delay_ms`, and the guardian cancels it by confirming (any vote counts); the owner can cancel too | Mitigated |
| 53 | Stolen guardian key rotates the seat away | A thief with a guardian key moves the seat to an account they control, locking the real guardian out | The thief already holds that seat's vote, so no new power; the rotation waits the swap delay, is announced, and the owner can cancel and replace the guardian | Mitigated (bounded) |
| 54 | Queued edit lands after the owner is gone | An attacker's (or stale) queued change applies after the owner stopped checking in | It applies only after the full notice, during which guardians and heirs are notified; it can't apply while a release is open, so it never changes a release in flight | Accepted (by design: a queued change is the owner's stated wish) |
| 55 | Standby collusion | Standbys wait for guardians to go quiet and take over the quorum | Standbys are named by the owner; each swap needs a guardian past the availability window, waits the swap delay and is announced; the quorum and seat count never change | Mitigated (bounded) |
| 56 | Replayed destination proof | A signature made for one plan, recipient or address is submitted for another | The signed text names the contract, plan owner, recipient, chain and exact address; the contract rebuilds it and checks the signature against the address | Mitigated |
| 57 | Heir (or a stolen heir key) redirects its share | Someone with a heir's key requests a destination they control | A request changes nothing by itself: only the owner can approve, approval is subject to the edit notice, and requests are accepted only while the owner is active | Mitigated |
| 58 | Request spam | A recipient floods the owner with requests | One request per recipient and chain (a new one replaces the old); only plan recipients may ask | Mitigated (bounded) |
| 59 | Owner loses their account | The owner can't check in or edit, so a release eventually starts while they're alive | Out of protocol scope: the plan follows the owner's NEAR account. Guidance (D31): a second full-access key or recovery phrase, check-in delegates, a DAO/multisig owner. An Ethereum-wallet owner can't add keys | Accepted (documented) |
| 60 | Heirs and shares readable on-chain | Family or strangers learn who inherits and how much while the owner is alive | D33 sealed plans store only salted commitments; details are revealed only once the owner is overdue, and re-sealed if the owner shows up. Transfers themselves stay public on every chain | Mitigated (until release) |
| 61 | Lost recovery record | A sealed heir can never be revealed | The owner's explicit rule after the reveal window (redistribute or fallback); optional encrypted backup with Behest; the heir packet carries the record | Mitigated (owner's choice) |
| 62 | Forged or replayed record | Someone reveals a record they invented, or one from another plan | The commitment binds contract, owner, account, share, destinations and a 256-bit salt; only an exact preimage matches | Mitigated |
| 63 | Front-run reveal | Someone copies a record from the mempool and submits it first | Harmless: the record's destinations are fixed, so the copier gains nothing | Mitigated |
| 64 | Premature reveal | A guardian or heir reveals while the owner is merely late, exposing the details | Reveals only open once the plan is overdue (after check-in and grace); the owner's next check-in re-seals, but the revealed details stay in transaction history | Accepted (documented) |
| 65 | Behest backup misuse | The record copy Behest keeps is read or released to the wrong person | Encrypted at rest; released only to the heir's own signed-in account (NEP-413 or EIP-191) and only once the plan is overdue; opt-in per plan | Mitigated (trust in Behest's service, opt-in) |
| 66 | Heir's inbox taken over (D34) | Whoever reads the email of a person named by email can sign in as them, claim, and move what arrives | The contract still decides when and how much: nothing is claimable before guardians approve and the waiting period ends, and the owner can cancel until then; the owner can name a wallet account instead, or other destinations; the app says plainly that the account is as safe as the inbox | Accepted (the owner's choice per person; documented in the app) |
| 67 | Wallet service compromised or gone (D34) | Privy signs for an email account without its owner, or stops serving it | Only people named by email are exposed, and only for what was paid to those accounts; the release itself needs no third party. A heir can move a received share to their own wallet at once. If the service vanished before a claim, the owner (while active) can rename the person; after that the unclaimed share follows the plan's alternates and fallback | Accepted (documented); alternates/fallback are the recovery path |
| 68 | Email-to-account lookups (D34) | Someone learns which on-chain account belongs to an email address | Only an owner signed in to the notifier gets the account for an address, rate-limited per owner; the public "make sure this address has wallets" call returns nothing about them; emails never go on-chain | Mitigated (an owner can always learn the accounts of people they name) |
| 69 | Owner-supplied address used to pester someone (D34) | An owner enters a stranger's email to make Behest write to them | Nothing is sent until a plan on-chain names the account for that address; then at most that plan's notices; one-click unsubscribe that the owner can't undo by naming them again; per-owner caps; the owner's display name is limited to plain letters so it can't carry a link | Mitigated |
| 70 | Bad send-out transaction (D34) | The page builds a NEAR or Bitcoin transfer that pays the wrong amount or address, or the service returns a bad signature | Destinations are validated for the network before signing; the Bitcoin fee is re-derived from the built transaction and must equal the one shown; signatures are verified against the account's key before use; tests compare the externally-signed transaction byte-for-byte with one a wallet holding the key produces | Mitigated |
